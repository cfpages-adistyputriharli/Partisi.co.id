## Purpose

This file is the shared pre-writing evidence layer for the complete partisi.co.id catalog. It supports interior and semi-exterior partition systems, including board-and-stud walls, glass/aluminium partitions, decorative screens, wet-area cubicles, doors, and movable/operable systems.

It is not an article, a replacement for a licensed standard, an engineering calculation, a project specification, or permission to publish definitive safety/compliance claims. Article writers must open and cite the original sources below, not this research file.

## How writers must use this file

1. Start with the coverage row for the relevant `PAR-xx` family and the article-suffix intent map.
2. Open the cited original source before using a technical, legal, product, or safety claim.
3. Treat a catalog page or public abstract as proof of document identity, status, and visible scope only. Obtain the full standard before quoting requirements, tolerances, classifications, test values, or pass/fail criteria.
4. Keep three layers separate: raw material, processed panel/product, and complete installed partition system. A board or laminate property does not prove the performance of framing, anchors, joints, doors, seals, penetrations, or interfaces.
5. Keep Indonesian requirements separate from ISO, ASTM, FEMA, US manufacturer, and other foreign guidance. Foreign sources are methods or practice references, not Indonesian law.
6. Name the exact tested or declared configuration behind any acoustic, fire, impact, moisture, hygiene, accessibility, or structural claim.
7. Preserve stop-work conditions and every professional-review gate in outlines and briefs.
8. Recheck laws, standards status, product documents, test reports, availability, and links at outline approval, and always when more than six months have passed since the verification date.

### KR-15 — Moisture, condensation, wetting, and mold control

- **Sources:** [US EPA Moisture Control Guidance for Building Design, Construction and Maintenance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/moisture-control-guidance-building-design-construction-and-maintenance-0), [Gypsum Association mold-prevention guidance](https://www.gypsum.org/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/GA-238-03.pdf), [Formica Compact fabrication guidance for wet areas](https://www.formica.com/zh-cn/-/media/project/formica/asia/documents/products-documents/compact/farbiration-guide_compact_2023.pdf?rev=692c868288044704aac2458e42c2bb23)
- **Grade:** C for government/industry concepts; D for the named compact product.
- **Purpose:** Ground moisture-exposure classification, material storage, ventilation, edge/interface details, leak diagnosis, and repair/replace decisions.
- **Summary:** EPA treats moisture control as a building design, construction, and maintenance task. Gypsum guidance says board must be kept dry and moisture sources corrected. Formica guidance says its compact material is moisture-resistant but still changes dimension with humidity and is not recommended for continuous immersion.
- **Grounded facts:** “Moisture resistant” is not “waterproof assembly.” Leaks, condensation, capillary contact, submerged/flood exposure, drainage, ventilation, edges, fastener holes, substrate, sealants, corrosion, and drying access can control service life and hygiene.

- **Limits/recheck:** US/industry guidance is not an Indonesian code. Exact exposure category, replacement threshold, sealant, chemical, and drying/remediation method need site facts and product instructions; resolve `G-05`, `G-06`, and `G-11`.

### KR-22 — Sustainability, adaptability, disassembly, and environmental claims

### KR-23 — Retrofit, demolition, and hazardous legacy materials

- **Sources:** [WHO asbestos fact sheet, 27 September 2024](https://www.who.int/westernpacific/newsroom/fact-sheets/detail/asbestos), [ILO position on safety in the use of asbestos](https://www.ilo.org/resource/ilo-position-safety-use-asbestos), [PP No. 22 Tahun 2021 environmental and waste-management framework — BPK](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/161852/pp-no-22-tahun-2021.html)

- **Purpose:** Establish a stop-before-disturbance gate for old boards, insulation, adhesives, coatings, contaminated wet areas, and unknown demolition waste.

## Final claim rules for outline writers

- Do not write “sesuai SNI,” “tahan api X menit,” “STC/Rw X,” “waterproof,” “antibacterial,” “aman gempa,” “aksesibel,” or a universal height/thickness/anchor/spacing unless the relevant gate is resolved and the exact evidence object is named.
- Use “method scope,” “catalog status,” “manufacturer-declared,” “laboratory result,” and “field result” precisely; they are not interchangeable.
- If a source supplies an exact number for one named foreign product, keep the product, market, configuration, report, and limitation attached to the number.
- A substitute material or component is a change to the evidence chain. Recheck support, compatibility, fire/acoustic/impact evidence, finish, maintenance, warranty, and approvals.
- Diagnosis articles may teach observation and recordkeeping, but they must not instruct unsafe opening, cutting, glass handling, powered-system servicing, fire-system alteration, suspect-material sampling, or structural repair.
- Procurement articles should teach comparable scope and evidence, not invent current prices, supplier capacity, warranty coverage, service areas, or legal conclusions.
